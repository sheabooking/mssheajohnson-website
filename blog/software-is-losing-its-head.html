Software Is Losing Its Head

(And why that’s good news for busy business owners)

Shea Johnson · October 2026 · 3 min read

When I first started using AI tools back in December 2022, everything felt familiar.

Open the browser.
Type in the web address.
Wait for the homepage to pop up.
Create an account.

Same routine I’d done a thousand times.

Then weeks later, everyone was talking about another AI tool.
And another.
And another.

I watched a simple chat conversation turn into images…
then videos…
then a clone of my own voice.

Then one day the friend next to me showed me a video on social media and asked,
“Is this AI or not?”

That’s when it hit me.
We weren’t just using new tools anymore.
The tools were starting to work without us clicking anything at all.

So what is headless software?

Here’s the simplest way I can say it.

For years, software had a “head”: the screen.
The buttons. The menus. The dashboards we log into and click around.

Headless software keeps the engine and lets go of the screen.

Instead of you clicking from app to app, site to site, an AI assistant connects to your tools behind the scenes and does the work for you.

You just tell it what you want.
Out loud, if you like.

The tech world is starting to call this shift headless. A popular a16z podcast episode even asked it out loud: “Is software losing its head?”

I think the answer is yes.
And honestly? It’s already in my house.

What it looks like in my business

I’m a small business owner.
I’m not a big tech company with a team of engineers.

This year, I figured out how to set up a team of AI agents to run my own business behind the scenes.

One handles my email.
One books my calendar.
One is organizing 20 years of my digital files and family photos, a project I thought would take me another five years to even start.
We’re almost halfway done.

And I talk to them by voice, from my phone.

Here’s the strange part.
It now feels weird to log in and check my own email.
I never imagined I’d feel that way.

Why I’m sharing this now

Not long ago, a business owner got in my car and told me he’d been searching online for someone who could set up AI agents for his business. Agents that could take care of things while he was asleep.

He said he didn’t even know where to start.

I remember feeling exactly like that.

That’s why I love sharing what I’ve learned with other business owners, especially women who run their business alone and on the go. The florist prepping orders before sunrise. The pottery artist with clay on her hands. The candle maker packing boxes at her kitchen table.

You don’t need to be technical.
You just need to be curious.

Because the future of AI isn’t years away.
It’s already here, working behind the scenes.
